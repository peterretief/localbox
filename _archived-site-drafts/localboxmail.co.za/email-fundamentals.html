2. Addresses and domains

In person@example.com, the local part identifies the mailbox and the domain identifies the administrative mail domain. The visible From address, SMTP envelope sender and authenticated account can be different, which matters during delivery and spam checks.

3. SMTP

SMTP transfers a message from a client to a submission server and between mail servers. The conversation is a sequence of commands and numeric replies.

S: 220 mail.example ESMTP ready
C: EHLO sender.example
S: 250-STARTTLS
C: MAIL FROM:<sender@example.com>
C: RCPT TO:<recipient@example.net>
C: DATA
C: Subject: Test message
C:
C: Hello
C: .
S: 250 queued

A 250 reply normally means that stage succeeded. A 4xx reply is temporary; a 5xx reply is normally permanent unless the configuration changes.

4. DNS and MX records

The sending server queries the recipient domain's MX records. The lowest preference number is tried first. The MX target must resolve to an address that accepts SMTP connections.

dig MX example.net
dig A mail.example.net
dig AAAA mail.example.net
nc -vz mail.example.net 25

MX records identify receiving hosts; they do not point directly to an IP address.

5. SPF, DKIM and DMARC

SPF

Publishes which servers are allowed to send using a domain in the SMTP envelope.

DKIM

Adds a cryptographic signature that the recipient verifies with a DNS public key.

DMARC

Defines alignment and policy when SPF or DKIM do not authenticate the visible From domain.

dig TXT example.com
dig TXT selector._domainkey.example.com
dig TXT _dmarc.example.com

Passing one mechanism is not automatically enough for DMARC; alignment with the visible From domain matters.

6. Mailboxes, IMAP and POP3

After the receiving server accepts and stores a message, a mail client usually synchronizes it with IMAP. IMAP keeps messages and folders on the server. POP3 is simpler and traditionally downloads messages to the client.

ProtocolTypical secure portPurpose
SMTP submission587Authenticated sending by users
IMAP over TLS993Mailbox synchronization
POP3 over TLS995Mailbox download

7. TLS and common ports

STARTTLS begins as a plain protocol connection and upgrades it to TLS. Implicit TLS starts encryption immediately. Do not confuse encryption in transit with sender authentication.

openssl s_client -starttls smtp -connect mail.example.com:25
openssl s_client -starttls smtp -connect mail.example.com:587
openssl s_client -connect mail.example.com:993

8. Troubleshooting workflow

  1. Read the complete SMTP reply and identify the server that produced it.
  2. Check the recipient MX record.
  3. Test whether TCP port 25 is reachable.
  4. Check forward DNS and reverse DNS.
  5. Inspect SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
  6. Inspect the sending queue and logs.
  7. Separate temporary deferrals from permanent rejections.
dig MX recipient.example
nc -vz mx.recipient.example 25
grep -i 'recipient@example' /var/log/mail.log
mailq

9. Suggested exercises

  1. Draw the envelope sender, visible From address and recipient for a sample message.
  2. Look up a domain's MX records and resolve each MX host.
  3. Explain the difference between SMTP submission and server-to-server SMTP.
  4. Inspect SPF, DKIM and DMARC records for a domain you control.
  5. Classify sample SMTP replies as temporary or permanent failures.
